Theme of the day: The price of money, and the price of a say. The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate a quarter point on Wednesday to 3.75%-4%, unanimously, its first tightening in three years — and the projections say core PCE does not reach the 2% target until 2029. Hours later the SEC proposed rescinding Rule 14a-8, handing shareholder proposals back to state law and company documents, on the heels of its move to make quarterly reporting optional. Read together, one raises the cost of capital and the other lowers the cost of being a public company, and both push in the same direction: the discount rate in your model goes up, and the gap between how a listed company and a sponsor-owned company are governed keeps narrowing. On the LP side, a $26bn pension with roughly half its assets in privates is publicly asking whether that tilt paid, while a former allocator is launching her own secondaries shop on the view that institutions have the capital but not the capacity to execute. South Africa gets the same question from two angles: an outgoing Growthpoint CEO’s exit as a study in capital discipline, and a Reserve Bank watching an oil spike undo easing inflation expectations.
