Theme of the day: The gate the decision has to pass through. Deloitte asked 1,434 finance leaders how the biggest technology spend actually gets approved, and roughly a quarter said it is not approved by any process at all — it arrives as a mandate from the C-suite or the board and finance funds it. That is the single most useful number on this list, because 54% of those same people say they are the ones leading the capital allocation. Pair it with the Prophix interview, which names the reason so many of those cases fall apart: “this saves the team ten hours a week” is not a financial return, because nobody fires a team for ten hours a week. On the private equity side, two stories about the shape capital arrives in rather than the amount — Portage closing a flagship at $600m against $655m last time while the platform around it grew to $7bn, and CPP Investments and Brookfield building a C$50bn deployment framework instead of raising a fund. In South Africa, Tim Cohen argues that Parks Tau’s R100bn transformation fund may have accidentally created a route out of the BEE scorecard rather than a tighter one, and Wandile Sihlobo points at the intra-Brics agricultural trade South Africa is not doing while the US tariff threat sits overhead.
