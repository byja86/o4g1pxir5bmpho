Theme of the day: What the thing actually costs, and who is allowed to see the price. Two US companies filed the real numbers for a finance chief this week: Adient is paying its incoming CFO a $820,000 base, a target bonus of 100% of that and a $4.2m equity award, while Hub Group handed its incoming CFO a $1.3m stock award and is paying its interim finance chief $175,000 a month plus a $1.2m retention bonus. Those are not gossip, they are a price list for the seat you are aiming at, and they tell you where the money in that job really sits. On the private equity side the same question runs underneath both items: Siguler Guff raised a record $3bn for US small buyouts, but almost $700m of it came through separately managed accounts rather than the fund itself, so the headline fund size understates the business — and Morgan Stanley closed a $1.3bn debut growth fund having committed close to a quarter of the capital itself, which is a very expensive way of answering the only question a first-time manager ever gets asked. In South Africa, A2 Investment Partners is the reason Phil Roux is being talked about at Spar: a minority stake bought quietly, a CEO installed, a 180% share price move at Nampak, and now the same template pointed at a struggling retailer. And Nersa has spent fifteen months refusing to say what seven large industrial users actually pay for electricity, which is the opposite case — a price deliberately kept off the table while everyone else pays the tariff. Today’s CPD is ACCA’s “Cash is king” five-course pack — 5 verifiable units, GBP 99, CPD certificate on completion — and working capital is the most underrated thing on this list, because it is where a deal’s returns are quietly won or lost.
