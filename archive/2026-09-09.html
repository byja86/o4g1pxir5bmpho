Theme of the day: Every item below is really about the same question — does value come from holding an asset, or from changing it? Apollo answers it hardest: it bought Kelvion in January, repointed a diversified industrial cooling business at data centres, and agreed to sell it to SLB for $4.1bn about seven months later. Redefine answers it slowly: nearly a decade of underperformance, then a deleveraging and a simplification, and the share is up more than 60% in eighteen months. The Hyper 5 are the open case — $1.1trn of capex already spent and $5.3trn forecast to 2030, with Amazon free cash flow at negative $7.6bn and Meta down 91%, so the reposition is real but the return is not yet provable. Sysco is paying $3m of performance shares to keep two people in their seats through a $29bn one, which tells you the market prices the execution, not the plan. Today’s CPD sits precisely on that fault line: eight verifiable ACCA units on corporate finance governance — the discipline that decides whether a reposition is an argument or a punt.
