Theme of the day: Whose calendar is the decision actually on. The best thing on this list is Mike Donaldson of RMB Corvest in the Financial Mail, explaining why selling a mid-sized South African business now takes nine to twelve months instead of four to six — and then naming the thing most people never say out loud: a closed-end fund has an eight or ten-year life, so sometimes a sale gets forced “because the fund’s calendar says it’s time, not the business”. RMB Corvest invests off its own balance sheet, has held Fidelity Security for about 35 years against an industry average nearer ten, and has sold at roughly three times cost across more than 200 transactions. He is also honest about the cost of that freedom: no forcing function can make for a lazier balance sheet. Read it beside Blackstone carving the Asian business out of Clarion Events to get a stalled disposal moving again — same problem, opposite solution. The CFO items are the internal version of the same question: a CFO seat filled in the middle of a governance fight, and a quietly excellent piece on what happens when every assumption in the model passes its own test and the total still makes no sense. In South Africa, the Reserve Bank decides tomorrow with August CPI landing the same morning. Today’s CPD is ACCA’s Accountant’s Update Programme 2026 — 22 verifiable units across eleven months, GBP 450 for members, certificate on completion — which is the whole annual requirement in one bookable line, and the right thing to buy before Red Badger starts.
