Daily Brief

Theme of the day: Concentration — who holds the capital, who holds the customer, and who is watching the risk. Two AltAssets pieces filed on the same morning tell one story: Goldman Sachs Alternatives closed buyout fund nine at $9.6bn, a hair under the $9.7bn predecessor, while PitchBook counts a fifth straight annual decline in private capital fundraising with almost four-fifths of all commitments now going to funds of $1bn or more. Flat is the new up, and only if you are already big. The South African pair is the same lesson wearing an apron: Beyers Chocolates had one customer worth R320m of about R650m in turnover, lost it in January 2025, collapsed owing Absa R235m — and Cartoon Candy has just bought the machines out of the liquidation and gone looking for that very customer. Kenny Fihla is fifteen months into fixing a two-decade identity crisis at Absa with a plan he sums up as simpler, better, faster. On the CFO side, EY finds boards moving risk oversight off the risk committee and onto the whole board, and a new survey finds one in ten procurement chiefs sharing no metrics at all with their CFO — both are about who is actually holding the exposure.
