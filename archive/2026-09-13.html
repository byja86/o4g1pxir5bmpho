Theme of the day: Capital allocation — who gets the next rand, at what price, and whether the number on the page is actually available to spend. Sergey Kyunttsel’s line is the spine of the whole brief: an approved project can still clear its hurdle and no longer be the best use of scarce capital, so reapproval should test the ranking, not only the spreadsheet. TFG is what happens when nobody does that — shares bought back by the company have since halved, and Rob Rose reports shareholders being overridden on pay at the same time. Eskom is the other failure mode: a R30bn profit headline that Michael Avery says is far less robust than it sounds once debt service, municipal arrears and the capital programme have taken their cut. Adobe is the opposite bet, deliberately carrying 100 million free users on the assumption that engagement converts to ARR. On the deal side you get both sides of the table for once: Apogem hard-capping fund eleven at $597m with its own staff committed, and Connecticut splitting $750m two ways against an allocation model rather than a pitch deck. Today’s CPD is the enabling skill: fifteen verifiable units, ACCA’s own certificate, assessed by exam — because none of these rankings can be made honestly by someone who cannot get at the data themselves.
